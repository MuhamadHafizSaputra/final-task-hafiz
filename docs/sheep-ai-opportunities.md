# Four AI opportunities for the sheep-weighing capstone

## Follow-up on 23 November 2026 — AI for the farmer's app

Hafiz confirmed that the marketplace is a later product. This follow-up evaluates four optional farmer-facing features. Choose at most one after observing the partner's routine. The original comparison below is retained as research history.

**What farmers do.** Firman et al. studied 61 Garut sheep farmers. Their daily activity diagram includes cleaning the pen, collecting grass, supplying forage in the morning and afternoon, and checking animals. In a separate sheep performance-recording experiment, manual entry and subsequent upload took longer than automated weight recording. These studies point to two different opportunities: interpreting observations during animal handling, and recording useful details with little extra work. A breeding flock, a fattening unit, and a grazing flock may have different priorities. ## Earlier comparison — 22 November 2026

These candidates address decisions made by a scale operator, marketplace support worker, buyer, or flock manager. Each can be tested as one app feature. The [original scope](project-context.md) places the marketplace outside the current thesis deliverables. This earlier review used a future sheep marketplace as the product context. Species, data access, and the supervisor's novelty criterion still need confirmation.

## 1. Find which recorded weights need review after a scale fails a check

**User and decision.** An operator weighs a series of sheep and then finds that a known check weight reads high. Some of the sheep may already have left. Which earlier records should the operator mark for review? Even a perfectly steady reading can be wrong when the scale's zero has shifted. A single mistaken measurement can otherwise appear on the app, a receipt, and a listing.

**Precedent and candidate contribution.** Sheep weigh stations have been validated against manual weighing [1]. Datamars documents automatic zeroing [2], and a livestock-weighing patent explicitly anticipates material accumulating on the platform [3]. Industrial checkweigher guidance already describes investigating everything measured since the last successful check [4]. The candidate is a *retrospective estimate of which identified sheep records were affected* and an evaluation of the unnecessary recalls it avoids. This use of a change-point model estimates when the offset began and how uncertain that estimate is. Preserve the original weights and expose the evidence to the operator.

**Inputs and practical limit.** Ask Lutfi to record brief unloaded windows before and after each pass, including their timestamps and raw readings before any automatic zeroing, plus changes of tare and known-mass checks. Confirm that the platform really was empty. Start with an additive offset. An empty scale can still read zero when the calibration slope is wrong. For that failure, known masses spanning the working range are needed. When the onset cannot be identified, present the entire interval since the last passing check for review.

**Test.** On a bench, introduce known offsets at recorded times with test masses and check them against independent reference measurements. Fit the model on separate runs, then evaluate unseen runs or days. Compare with flagging the whole interval and with a simple empty-platform threshold. Report affected records missed, valid records unnecessarily flagged, and time-to-onset error. Report real farm traces and bench faults separately. The feature earns its place only if it narrows the review while still finding affected records. A bench result alone establishes technical feasibility.

## 2. Help resolve a difference between dispatch weight and pickup weight

**User and decision.** A seller and a buyer weigh the same sheep several hours apart and get different values. Support needs to decide whether to request a comparable reweigh. The marketplace can generate the first record from the scale and link it to a later check by animal ID. This is a concrete customer-support task even if farmers never open an analytics dashboard.

**Precedent and candidate contribution.** A sheep study documented weight changes during ordinary handling and waiting [5]. Researchers have already developed corrections for delayed ewe weighing [6]. AuctionsPlus explicitly handles weight disputes, including reweighing and accounting for differences in weighing conditions [7]. Its delivery form also records weighing terms [8]. A generic shrink calculator or dispute workflow would repeat existing work. The candidate is to learn an expected *range* for an individual handover from local, independently verified pairs and test whether it helps support request the right rechecks. This is strongest when the local transaction is for one identified animal and disagreement has a real consequence.

**Inputs and practical limit.** Collect paired same-sheep weights, both times and device IDs, check-weight results for both devices, and observable handling conditions such as time since gathering. Train on routine handovers as well as reported discrepancies. Missing conditions, unfamiliar routes, or a second unverified scale should produce “insufficient evidence.” The tool can suggest a reweigh. It cannot determine fault or what anyone owes.

**Test.** Use a simple percentage allowance and a time-only regression as baselines for a model estimating lower and upper expected pickup weights. Hold out complete consignments or routes. Test interval coverage and width, unnecessary rechecks, and large discrepancies missed. For operational evaluation, establish which cases warranted rechecking through an independent repeat measurement and documented review. If the partner never weighs at handover and has no way to collect paired records, this candidate has no local training or evaluation target.

## 3. Check whether the sheep collected is the sheep on the order

**User and decision.** A dispatch worker or buyer can use a phone to check an order when there is no RFID reader at pickup or an ear-tag number cannot be read. When the sheep is weighed for its listing, the seller captures a reference photo tied to the verified tag. At handover the app compares a new photo with that one animal and can request a clearer view or a manual tag check.

**Precedent and candidate contribution.** Indonesia's Goopo already addresses customer concerns about animals being mixed up by linking a unique physical tag to transaction documents [9]. An Indonesian sheep-selection project at Arjuna Farm documents the information needs of remote buyers [10]. Sheep facial identification [11], visual identification using different body regions and tag text [12], and biometric identity auditing in lambs [13] already exist. The narrower question is whether a phone-photo fallback on a specific order reduces handover errors or delays compared with reading the visible tag. This candidate has particularly close prior art.

**Inputs and test.** Use ordinary phone photos from two separate sessions, with verified identity at both, similar-looking sheep, different devices, and genuine mismatched pairs. A pretrained image model can compare visual features and abstain below a validation threshold. Hide tag text when testing the visual comparison, otherwise the model could simply read the answer. Keep sheep identities and photo sessions separate across training and final testing. Report wrong sheep accepted, genuine matches sent for manual checking, and handover time against a tag or QR check. Count independent sheep as well as photo pairs. If visible tags already settle every handover quickly, this feature adds work.

## 4. Tell whether the flock got lighter or a different group was weighed

**User and decision.** Suppose the average on a pen dashboard drops after several heavier sheep are sold. A manager may reasonably think the remaining animals lost weight. The app could instead report the weight change among comparable animals, which IDs entered or left, and whether coverage is sufficient to interpret the trend. This uses weighing and stock movements already generated by routine operations.

**Precedent and candidate contribution.** Sheep studies have evaluated the frequency of individual scale visits [14], the precision of flock averages [15], and detection of nutrition-related changes from repeated ewe weights [16]. AgriWebb displays weight trends [17]. A patent describes comparisons between animal and cohort growth [18]. The candidate is to evaluate how often accounting for changing pen membership and incomplete coverage prevents *false flock-loss alerts* while preserving genuine alerts. The app would provide a reason to check a pen, without attributing a cause such as diet or disease.

**Inputs and test.** Collect a dated pen roster with joins, sales and transfers, repeat ID-linked weights, and occasional complete-pen reference sessions. First compare the raw mean with the median change for sheep present on both dates. A learned model can estimate individual growth trajectories and uncertainty when visits are irregular. Show “insufficient overlap” if the roster and observations cannot support the inference. Use complete sessions to establish true change for continuing animals, then mask selected records to reproduce observed coverage patterns. Evaluate on separate pen-weeks, measure false alerts and missed real declines, and check the result on naturally incomplete weeks where follow-up exists. If the simple matched-animal comparison works just as well, it is the better implementation.

## Which idea to take to the partner

| Priority | One question that could change the decision | Minimum evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scale audit, best hardware fit | “After a scale check failed, what happened to the animals already weighed?” | Access to the device, unloaded readings and known masses. A farm example establishes practical relevance. |
| Dispatch discrepancy, best marketplace fit | “Show me the last time pickup weight differed from the seller's weight. Who decided what to do?” | Two verified weighings of the same sheep under recorded conditions across several independent handovers. |
| Identity check, conditional on tag failures | “How often is the tag unreadable or unavailable at pickup, and how is identity checked then?” | Verified photos from different sessions and a realistic tag-based comparison. |
| Flock trend, strongest management option | “Can you show two pen reports where sales or missing animals changed the average?” | Time-stamped pen membership, repeated weights, and complete reference sessions. |
