# AI opportunities for the goat-weighing capstone

Research note, 22 November 2026. The AI direction below is a candidate for discussion with the farm, Lutfi, and the supervisor. The [project scope](project-context.md) records the existing decision about recovering an interrupted weighing.

## Which goats should the farmer weigh next?

Imagine a farmer has time to bring five goats to the scale before deciding which animals meet a buyer's liveweight target. Yesterday's records are of different ages. Some goats were well above or below the target. Others were close, growing at different rates, or difficult to weigh reliably. A new measurement can change the decision for some individuals and add little for others. The proposed app would give the farmer five IDs, an explanation for each choice, and a place to record the actual weight. A prediction should remain visibly distinct from a measured weight.

There is evidence for the underlying workload problem. In a study of five Dutch dairy-goat farms and over 700 kids, farmers valued individual growth records, yet two farms returned incomplete or poor-quality datasets. Workload during kidding was a reported barrier [1]. An Australian goat-production guide explicitly discusses weighing a recurring sample when monitoring a large flock [2]. Indonesian research provides two pieces of local context. An Etawa-crossbreed communal farm already tested automated weighing with a mobile app [3], and a Semarang study reported an association between liveweight and price per head for qurbani goats [4]. The commercial decision, handling bottleneck, and ability to retrieve named animals at the partner farm still need direct observation. Dairy kid rearing and preparing goats for sale are different workflows.

**Closest precedent.** Stygar and Kristensen used selected pens and a growth model to estimate the number of pigs above a delivery threshold. They explicitly evaluated the value of additional weights [5]. Cotticelli and colleagues forecast individual lamb weights from daily scale visits [6]. AgriWebb already forecasts livestock weights against sale targets using actual or assumed daily gain [7]. A published patent application describes projecting shipment timing from livestock records [8]. These sources substantially narrow the possible contribution.

**Candidate contribution.** Test whether an application can select individual goats whose physical weighing would prevent the most wrong above/below-target decisions, given a farmer-defined limit on handling. Test whether past weighing quality adds value beyond weight history, time since last weighing, and proximity to the target. I did not identify a goat field evaluation of that specific decision in the public sources reviewed. That finding is a research lead. Switching species, adding a button, showing a forecast, and combining sensors with an app already have close precedents.

**Possible proposal wording in Indonesian.** “Proyek ini akan merancang dan mengevaluasi rekomendasi prioritas penimbangan kambing berdasarkan riwayat bobot dan ketidakpastian prediksi. Evaluasi akan mengukur apakah rekomendasi mengurangi kesalahan penentuan status bobot terhadap target peternak pada jumlah penimbangan yang sama.”

## What the app would learn and how to test it

1. **Establish the decision.** Observe one weighing or sorting session. Record why the farmer weighs, how many goats are eligible, how many can be handled, how animals are chosen now, and what happens if an animal is wrongly classified. Ask whether the farmer wants an accurate status for the herd or wants to find a fixed number of qualifying animals quickly. Those objectives produce different lists. If the operator already weighs every animal on the way through the gate or cannot retrieve a named goat, the proposed list has little operational value.
2. **Audit the usable data.** Count identifiable goats, distinct weighing dates per goat, time between visits, follow-up duration, and examples on both sides of the farm's target. Keep animal ID, timestamp, accepted weight, and how it was measured. Test previous pass quality or retry history as optional inputs where retained. Verify that these indicators predict measurement error against an independent weight. A noisy trace can still yield a good final weight. Ten readings per second in one pass supply one animal visit for a growth model.
3. **Build a simple baseline first.** Compare the farmer's present selection, longest time since weighing, and ranking goats nearest the target using last weight plus recent average daily gain. A candidate learned model could pool growth rates across goats while allowing individual differences and estimating how forecast error grows with time since weighing. Use those errors to estimate each goat's probability of exceeding the target on the session date. Estimate uncertainty from held-out forecasts and check calibration. A complex model needs to earn its place against these baselines.
4. **Turn the prediction into a decision.** If both kinds of mistake cost the same, each weighing costs roughly the same, and a reliable fresh weight resolves the status, the expected error removed by weighing a goat is the smaller of its probabilities of being above or below target. With probabilities of 98%, 52%, and 5% of reaching target, one available weighing goes to the 52% goat. If the farmer instead needs one qualifying goat immediately, the 98% goat is the first candidate. Show the rationale in the app. Give goats with no usable history an explicit first-weighing workflow and track its share of the available slots.
5. **Test the decision directly.** On evaluation dates collect trusted reference weights for eligible goats, including those the model would skip. Generate each ranked list using only information available before that session. Reveal the reference weights for the selected goats, then count wrong threshold decisions across the herd at the same weighing budget for each method. Record false-above and false-below separately. Repeat across dates and report goat and session counts, uncertainty, and performance for new goats. Use earlier dates for training and later dates for evaluation. Hold out entire animals if claiming transfer to new animals. Compare models with and without quality indicators. In an operator pilot also measure time spent locating and weighing the suggested animals.

**Go/no-go decision.** Continue only if the farmer confirms a consequential decision, a handling constraint, and access to repeated weights and evaluation sessions. If a simple rule matches the model, report that result. If only one visit per goat is available during the project, the data can support a weighing-system evaluation, while a local longitudinal AI claim would require additional records. The original recovery feature addresses a failed *current* pass. This candidate decides which animal to bring in on a *future* visit. Taking on both would require agreement on scope and ownership.

## What about predicting when a goat will give birth?

| Farmer's question | What research has demonstrated | What it would take here |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How many days until this doe gives birth? | A dairy-goat study estimated days remaining using ultrasound measurements of fetuses and actual kidding dates [9]. A separate Carpathian-goat study used individual mating and kidding registers to calculate gestation length [10]. | First test a calendar estimate based on recorded mating and typical gestation in the training herd. A candidate AI experiment would ask whether adding the doe's pre-birth weight history improves that estimate. Collect confirmed pregnancies, reliable mating dates where available, dated maternal weights, and actual kidding dates. Evaluate days of absolute error and coverage of the predicted date range on held-out pregnancies. |
| Should someone check on this doe in the next few hours? | Kim and colleagues used hind-leg movement, a decision tree, and an alarm terminal. They detected 14 of 17 births, on average about 91 minutes before the first kid among detections [11]. Another goat study found that short-horizon detection from inertial sensors remained difficult [12]. | This is an alert during a short window. The gate observes the doe only when she visits. An overnight birth may occur entirely between visits. To evaluate an alert, record actual birth times, alert lead time, missed births, and false alerts per monitored doe-day. Continuous wearables would change Lutfi's hardware and the project's scope. |

I found no validation in the reviewed sources for predicting the birth date or hour from intermittent maternal scale weights alone. That makes it a higher-risk research question. A real public goat-collar dataset exists [13], but its accelerometer and temperature signals differ from scale records. Kidding becomes the stronger direction if the partner is a breeding farm, farmers can act on an alert or date window, and usable histories cover enough independent births during the project. Count pregnancies before estimating what is feasible. Kid birth weight is a different outcome from date or time of birth.

## Other AI features with working precedents

| Feature | What already exists | Implication for the capstone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Smartphone health screening | A goat study implemented Android image scoring for eye-color assessment using TensorFlow Lite [14]. A separate sheep study built Android image acquisition and tested machine learning for anemia classification [15]. Another small-ruminant study analyzed images of blood spots [16]. | These are concrete examples of how an app can turn a specific input into an actionable output. Reproducing them would require images and independent reference labels outside the weighing workflow. |
| Weight anomaly alert | A cattle study combined RFID, load cells, ESP32 modules, a random forest, and a one-class support vector machine. Its health-event evaluation used simulated events [17]. | An unusual weight can prompt a confirmatory measurement. A disease-specific claim would require independently verified health outcomes. A generic anomaly flag already has close prior art. |
| Growth curve or estimated target date | Boer-goat growth curves have been fitted using ten nonlinear functions [18]. Lamb weights have been forecast from repeated daily measurements [6]. Commercial forecasting is available [7]. | These are realistic baselines or supporting app functions. A graph or an estimated target date alone would be difficult to defend as the main contribution. |

## Search scope

The search covered public journal and institutional repository records, publisher full text where available, agricultural extension publications, product documentation, a patent publication, and a dataset record. English and Indonesian queries covered goat kidding, parturition, gestation, maternal weight, birth alerts, growth forecasting, selected animal weighing, value of weight information, uncertainty, target liveweight, and *prioritas penimbangan kambing/domba*. Sheep, cattle, and pig studies were included where the decision or sensing system closely matched. This is a targeted prior-art review. Proprietary features and non-indexed theses may contain further precedents. The supervisor's novelty criterion is pending.
