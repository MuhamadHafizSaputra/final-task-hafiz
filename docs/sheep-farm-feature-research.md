# Additional sheep-farm problems for the weighing capstone

23 November 2026. These are candidates for field investigation. Hafiz’s three saved AI ideas appear at the top of the [capstone to-do list](capstone-todo.md). The marketplace is a separate project.

## 1. How much did the feed cost for each kilogram this pen gained?

**Farmer and decision.** A fattening farmer purchases concentrate and weighs sheep at intervals. The feed receipt says what a sack cost. The weight report says how much individual animals changed. The question for the next production review is how much the farmer spent on feed for the gain achieved by the same pen over the same period.

**Evidence.** A UGM study on 27 Garut sheep at Mendo Galak Farm in Sleman recorded daily gain, feed conversion and feed cost per gain. An IPB study at Kelompok Tani Ternak Alkadom in Sukabumi explicitly examined feed costs and growth in 23 group-housed sheep. These are research measurements, so confirm that the partner also makes a decision with them. BenguFarm already documents pen feed quantities, electronic scale imports, and growth and cost calculations. Cornell offers a lamb-feeding workbook. [UGM institutional abstract](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/207541) · [IPB institutional abstract](https://repository.ipb.ac.id/handle/123456789/83459) · [BenguFarm Feedlot Manager](https://www.bengufarm.co.za/index.php/sheep) · [Cornell Sheep Program workbook](https://blogs.cornell.edu/newsheep/cornell-sheep-program-software/)

**Feature.** Link accepted opening and closing weights to a stable pen roster. Record feed actually issued to that pen and the applicable price, rather than charging the pen for an entire delivery to the farm. Divide period feed cost by the sum of the same sheep’s weight gains. For example, Rp600,000 and 30 kg of group gain give Rp20,000 per kg gained. Label which costs are included, especially when grass is collected by family members. This describes observed feed expenditure per kilogram. Total profit, individual intake, and the causal effect of a ration are different quantities.

**Smallest credible test.** Reconstruct one completed feeding period from invoices or a farm ledger, pen issue logs, and matched weights. Compare with an independently checked spreadsheet on the same records. Then time the farmer producing the report by each method. Include an animal transfer, a missing final weight, and zero or negative net gain. Show the underlying records and withhold the ratio when the period cannot be reconciled. A pen that changes membership can be split into periods only when costs and boundary weights can be attributed accordingly. No AI needed.

**Commercial comparison for the pen calculation (23 November 2026).** AgriWebb documents feed cost divided by kilograms gained per individual animal, to date or over its recorded lifetime. Its mob reports cover feeding costs and gross margins. The published guides do not identify a matched pen-and-period ratio. Shearwell FarmWorks documents feed cost per animal and group profit margins. BreedELITE documents group weight-gain trends and feed conversion as a management objective. Neither vendor guide verifies the proposed pen ratio. Outside those three, Farmbrite publishes a working group-and-period calculator where the operator supplies group gain and feed cost. Compare the farmer’s effort to obtain the inputs and check which sheep contributed to the result. [AgriWebb individual report](https://help.agriwebb.com/en/articles/4409980-animal-performance-reports-iam) · [AgriWebb mob report](https://help.agriwebb.com/en/articles/3288458-livestock-cost-of-production-and-gross-margin-mob) · [Shearwell report guide](https://static.shearwell.com/Content/UK/Pdf/Support/Guides/FarmWorks/FarmWorks-Reports.pdf) · [BreedELITE software](https://breedelite.com/sheep-software/) · [Farmbrite calculator](https://www.farmbrite.com/post/cost-of-gain-calculator)

### Indonesian precedents and a candidate use of the scale data

The Indonesian scale and recording references reviewed do not explicitly document a farmer selecting a pen and period and getting feed expenditure per kilogram of matched liveweight gain. The IPB abstract describes weighing and age/weight clustering. The Wardhana et al. abstract describes feed and health histories. RUMINARA advertises feed costs, growth and cost of production in its fattening product. Indonesian researchers already calculate feed cost per gain: the Mendo Galak study included it for Garut sheep in Sleman, and a 63-day Bogor study reported Rp30,826.86/kg for its colony-housed group. These institutional abstracts describe research outcomes. Internationally, Iowa State’s Sheep Feedlot Monitor already reports period feed cost per pound of shrunk liveweight gain by feeding lot. [IPB](https://repository.ipb.ac.id/handle/123456789/163209) · [Wardhana et al.](https://journal.uinjkt.ac.id/index.php/ti/article/view/50110) · [RUMINARA](https://ruminara.id/trading-fattening) · [UGM](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/207541) · [Polije](https://sipora.polije.ac.id/41313/) · [Iowa State manual](https://storemedia.extension.iastate.edu/SecondaryFiles/AS7-HelpFile.pdf)

**Candidate to validate.** When a pen crosses the farmer's cost-per-gain threshold, could a different weighing schedule explain the alert? Feeding and waiting can affect liveweight even when the scale is steady. Earlier researchers evaluated correction equations for delayed sheep weighings. The proposed app would preserve measured weights, identify timing mismatches, and request a comparable weighing if plausible variation could reverse the threshold decision. Start with a before/after-feed flag and same-routine comparisons. If repeated paired observations exist, fit a small regression on local deviations and compare it with that rule on later, held-out sessions. Use a standardized routine reference to judge false alarms, missed alerts and extra weighing requests. [Wishart et al.](https://pure.sruc.ac.uk/files/15260823/14643.pdf) · [Ewe-lamb weighing experiment](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0472/11/2/150)

**Alternative if weighing times are already consistent.** Reprice the current period’s actual feed quantities with the previous period’s ingredient prices. Divide by the same current gain. The difference from actual Rp/kg measures the price contribution at the current quantities; the remaining difference can include quantity, mix, gain and measurement changes. Test whether a farmer can identify an invoice price change more quickly. Both options need feed issue dates, amounts, a traceable unit cost and credible pen membership. Pursue them only if the operator actually reviews feed spending against pen growth.

## 2. Which lambs can the farmer fairly compare at weaning?

**Farmer and decision.** A breeding farm weighs several lambs on the same date. The largest lamb may also be several weeks older. Birth and rearing circumstances can differ. The practical decision is which differences in the weight report merit a closer look.

**Evidence.** Garut sheep records from Margawati have been analysed with corrections for age at weaning, sex, and birth type. A Bogor farm study reported differences in weaning weight by sex and birth type and did not find a significant seasonal effect in that dataset. Sheep Ireland’s LambPlus scheme records management group, birth and rearing information, and accepts weights within age windows for subsequent age adjustment. The exact schedule and coefficients are specific to the population and scheme. [UGM study of Garut records, abstract](https://etd.repository.ugm.ac.id/penelitian/detail/110884) · [Bogor Garut study, institutional paper](https://repository.ipb.ac.id/jspui/bitstream/123456789/83458/1/60.Performa%20dan%20Evaluasi%20Genetik%20Bobot%20Lahir%20dan%20Bobot%20Sapih%20Domba%20Garut%20Dipeternakan%20Ternak%20Domba%20Sehat%20Bogor%20.pdf) · [Sheep Ireland recording timeline](https://www.sheep.ie/lambplus-recording-timeline/)

**Feature.** Beside the measured weight, show age in days, a comparable management group, and whether the lamb was born and reared as a single or multiple when known. The simplest version compares lambs within a locally appropriate age window and shows the number of peers. With weights on both sides of a partner-agreed reference age, test linear interpolation. Label the result as an estimate and keep the measured weights visible. Without credible birth dates or suitable measurements, display the available facts. Avoid a whole-flock ranking when comparability is poor.

**Smallest credible test.** Give the farm adviser the present weight list and the age-aware report for the same lamb crop. Record misinterpreted comparisons, unanswered cases, and time needed to identify animals for follow-up. If evaluating interpolation, hide a real observation at the reference age and compare the estimate with that measurement. Compare against the nearest available weight. Local records from enough independent lambs would be needed before a learned growth curve is worth evaluating. A flock that buys animals with unknown birth dates is a poor fit.

## 3. How many days of stored forage remain if the next supply is delayed?

**Farmer and decision.** A farmer who uses stored silage or another feed reserve needs to know whether it can bridge a delay in cutting grass or receiving a delivery. This concerns stock in storage across several days. It is a different observation from photographing leftovers in today’s trough.

**Evidence.** A Sarimukti community programme involving 25 Garut sheep farmers identified seasonal forage availability as a constraint and worked on local silage production. A Bogor study of 64 smallholders found that harvesting practices, flock size, collection containers, and feeding time were associated with forage supply; some farmers also used traders, home gardens, and paddy fields. AHDB publishes a calculator that compares livestock demand with available conserved forage. These sources establish a supply problem and an existing budgeting approach. An app can expose a shortfall early only when the operator can measure stock and act on the warning. [Sarimukti field programme](https://jurnal.unpad.ac.id/fjcs/article/download/69114/27275) · [Bogor smallholder survey, abstract](https://ojs.pps.unsri.ac.id/index.php/ppsunsri/article/view/308) · [AHDB feed and forage calculator](https://ahdb.org.uk/feed-and-forage-calculator)

**Feature.** For each feed type, record usable stock, deliveries and amounts issued in consistent units. Show days remaining at the recent rate and what happens if a planned delivery arrives late. Keep expected deliveries separate from stock already in the shed. Sheep headcount from an up-to-date roster can prompt a recalculation after a pen change. Using the animal scale as a feed scale is unnecessary. Begin with current stock divided by observed daily use. If issues vary, show a range based on recent high and low usage. Do not add unlike fresh and dry weights or treat a stock forecast as a feeding prescription.

**Smallest credible test.** Predict the next physical inventory count or the runout date for a specific reserve and compare with what staff actually measure, accounting for any deliveries. Compare prediction error and warning time with the farmer’s own estimate. A farm that collects all forage fresh each day and holds no reserve has a gathering problem that this feature would not solve. This option has the weakest direct connection to a weighing thesis.

## 4. How does a replacement tag inherit the right animal’s weight history?

**Farmer and decision.** A sheep loses its tag. Later a new tag scans successfully, but the old weighings are under a different number. Creating a fresh profile breaks the growth history. Guessing the old ID can join two sheep’s records and corrupt both histories.

**Evidence.** In a University of California extension case study, a small sheep producer reported loss of records associated with the old number when the previous lambing tag could not be read. The author’s tag-loss frequency was an estimate from one operation. In a separate Turkish field study of 380 lambs, both visual and electronic tags had 98% readability after a year. These findings justify asking about local loss frequency before building. iLivestock and AgriWebb already document replacement-tag workflows. [Producer case study](https://ucanr.edu/sites/default/files/2018-11/294788.pdf) · [Field study, Archives Animal Breeding](https://aab.copernicus.org/articles/58/287/2015/aab-58-287-2015.html) · [iLivestock replacement tags](https://help.ilivestock.com/en/articles/6055140-lost-and-replacement-sheep-tags) · [AgriWebb tagging records](https://help.agriwebb.com/en/articles/8454999-tagging-records-iam)

**Feature.** Keep the animal record independent of a physical tag. After a worker verifies identity from independent evidence such as a second identifier, associate the new tag with the same animal and date the change. Preserve the original scanned code on each weighing. A delayed upload captured before replacement should still resolve using the assignment valid at capture. If identity is unknown, hold the weighing for review. The shortlisted camera feature helps read a tag that is present; this feature addresses a tag that is physically gone.

**Smallest credible test.** Use known animals and labeled scenarios covering a confirmed replacement, an unknown former ID, an already-assigned replacement tag, and an old weighing uploaded after retagging. Compare history completeness, false merges, and correction time against the farm’s existing register or app. Staged cases establish technical feasibility; an actual replacement establishes relevance. No AI needed. If rare and well handled already, keep it a supporting feature.

## What to take to the partner meeting

Ask the fattening operator for one dated pen feed record and its matching weighings. Ask a breeder to show which lambs they compared at the last weaning. Ask whoever manages forage to show one reserve, its unit, and the last time supply ran short. Ask whoever tags sheep to walk through the last replacement. An actual decision, a data source and a feasible comparison are grounds to pursue one candidate. None has been selected for implementation.

### Which comparisons demonstrate scale-to-record transfer?

Two Indonesian projects narrow the novelty claim further. An IPB repository abstract (2025) reports an HX711 load cell, sheep RFID, an ESP32, and live transmission to a Flutter web app with MySQL storage. It describes grouping sheep using age and weight. Wardhana and colleagues (2026, journal abstract) report automatic weighing, cloud storage, a mobile app and feed and health histories on a communal goat farm. They report successful transmission for 20 monitoring records. The former is a direct sheep comparator for the measurement path. The latter is a close comparator for the broader farmer app on a related species. [IPB repository](https://repository.ipb.ac.id/handle/123456789/163209) · [Wardhana et al.](https://journal.uinjkt.ac.id/index.php/ti/article/view/50110)

### Animal identity in the proposed schema

The [animal and tag model](database.dbml) and [SQL schema](schema.sql) give an animal a permanent UUID. Each tag has assignment and removal times. Weighings, health records, treatments and parentage reference animal IDs. For a verified change from A to B, the application can close A and assign B to the same animal in one database transaction. Subsequent scans can open the existing profile and historical records retain their links. This describes what the schema supports. The user workflow and its results still need demonstrating.

Two details affect a fair comparison. First, the SQL makes the tag code globally unique. A second physical tag with the same number cannot be inserted as a new assignment row alongside the old row. If the farm uses same-number replacements, a dated replacement event referencing the existing code could preserve the distinction. Second, weighing sessions can store a resolved animal ID, but have no dedicated field for the tag as originally read. Check the device payload and storage contract before claiming that staff can audit an uncertain ID or resolve an earlier pass after retagging. Sheep Ireland already specifies event dates and old identifiers in its retagging exchange format. [Sheep Ireland file specification](https://www.sheep.ie/wp-content/uploads/2015/09/SheepIreland_FileUpload_format.pdf)

### Evidence to obtain before elevating this to the thesis contribution

Observe a single animal through a routine weighing and a later lookup by someone using its health, breeding or growth record. Compare the operator’s present method with the prototype on time to find the correct history, duplicate entry, and wrong or missing records. For retagging, ask what independent evidence lets staff recognize an animal with a lost tag. Test a confirmed change, an already-assigned tag, an unknown animal and a weighing captured before replacement but delivered afterwards. Record how often these situations actually occur. Treat a broad animal register as the product, and make any stronger novelty claim conditional on the partner’s unmet need, measurable results and the supervisor’s criterion.
